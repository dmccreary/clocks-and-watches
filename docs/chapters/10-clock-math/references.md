# References: Coordinates, Trigonometry, and Clock Geometry

1. [Radian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radian) - Wikipedia - Defines the radian as the angle whose arc equals the radius and relates it to degrees and 2 pi per turn. It supports the chapter's radians versus degrees conversions for clock hand angles.

2. [Polar coordinate system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_coordinate_system) - Wikipedia - Describes locating a point by distance and angle from a pole, and converting to x and y. It is the main idea behind the chapter's polar to screen formula for placing hands and ticks.

3. [Trigonometric functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigonometric_functions) - Wikipedia - Defines sine and cosine from right triangles and the unit circle, with graphs and identities. It gives the mathematical foundation for the chapter's sine function and cosine function sections.

4. Trigonometry - I. M. Gelfand and Mark Saul - Birkhauser - Motivates trigonometric functions geometrically before their algebra, with a chapter on radian measure after angles are treated as rotations. Its exact rotation treatment is less certain.

5. Trigonometric Delights - Eli Maor - Princeton University Press - Maor weaves history, biography, and mathematics, and an appendix argues for the unit circle's geometric definitions of sine and cosine. Chosen as a widely praised, readable text; no originator is claimed.

6. [math - mathematical functions](https://docs.micropython.org/en/latest/library/math.html) - MicroPython Documentation - Documents sin(), cos(), radians(), degrees(), and pi on the Pico. These are the exact functions the chapter uses to turn a time into a hand's x and y position.

7. [5.1 Angles](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus/pages/5-1-angles) - OpenStax Precalculus - A free textbook section on angles in standard position and converting between degrees and radians, including full rotation as 2 pi. It supports the chapter's angle calculation and rotation math.

8. [Unit Circle](https://www.mathsisfun.com/geometry/unit-circle.html) - Math Is Fun - An approachable illustrated page showing sine and cosine as the coordinates of a point on a radius-1 circle. It makes the chapter's unit circle explorer and hand-tip formula easier to see.

9. [Calculus II - Polar Coordinates](https://tutorial.math.lamar.edu/Classes/CalcII/PolarCoordinates.aspx) - Paul's Online Math Notes - Gives the formulas for converting between polar and Cartesian coordinates with worked examples. It confirms the conversion behind the chapter's center point, clock hand length, and tick mark geometry.

10. [Polar Coordinates](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/PolarCoordinates.html) - Wolfram MathWorld - A concise reference defining polar coordinates in terms of Cartesian x and y, with related formulas. It offers a compact check on the chapter's polar coordinates and angle calculation rules.
