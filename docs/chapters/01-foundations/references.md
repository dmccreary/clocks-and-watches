# References: Computational Thinking and Physical Computing

1. [Computational thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_thinking) - Wikipedia - Defines computational thinking and its core skills of decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and algorithm design. It gives students the vocabulary used throughout this chapter when they break a clock into smaller parts.

2. [Microcontroller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcontroller) - Wikipedia - Explains how a microcontroller packs a processor, memory, and input/output pins onto one chip. It supports this chapter's explanation of why a Pico can run a clock without a full computer.

3. [RP2040](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RP2040) - Wikipedia - Describes the Raspberry Pi RP2040 chip, including its two Arm Cortex-M0+ cores, on-chip SRAM, and programmable I/O. It backs up the chapter sections on the chip inside the Pico and dual core architecture.

4. Mindstorms: Children, Computers, and Powerful Ideas (2nd Edition) - Seymour Papert - Basic Books - Papert shows children programming a Logo turtle so that debugging and breaking a problem into steps become natural ways of thinking. Wing's 2006 article later popularized the term "computational thinking" itself.

5. Physical Computing: Sensing and Controlling the Physical World with Computers - Dan O'Sullivan and Tom Igoe - Thomson Course Technology - The authors teach interactive devices as systems that sense the world and respond, building on their physical computing course at NYU. This sense-and-respond framing matches the chapter's sensors, controller, and actuators model.

6. [Pico-series Microcontrollers](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/microcontrollers/pico-series.html) - Raspberry Pi Documentation - The official overview of the Pico boards, their RP2040 chip, wireless variants, board layouts, and supported software tools. Students can compare it with the Pico Board Explorer diagram in this chapter.

7. [RP2040 Datasheet](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf) - Raspberry Pi Datasheets - The manufacturer's complete reference for the RP2040, listing its dual cores, SRAM, clocks, and crystal oscillator requirements. Read selected sections to see how a real chip is specified, not to master every page.

8. [What is computational thinking?](https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/guides/zp92mp3/revision/1) - BBC Bitesize - A short, student-friendly introduction to decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and algorithms with everyday examples. It reinforces the four thinking skills in the first half of this chapter in plain language.

9. [Computational Thinking (Jeannette M. Wing, 2006)](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~15110-s13/Wing06-ct.pdf) - Carnegie Mellon University - The influential Communications of the ACM viewpoint arguing that computational thinking is a skill for everyone, not just computer scientists. It is the source most credited with popularizing the idea this chapter introduces.

10. [Getting Started with Raspberry Pi Pico and CircuitPython](https://learn.adafruit.com/getting-started-with-raspberry-pi-pico-circuitpython) - Adafruit Learning System - A clear overview of the Pico board, its RP2040 chip, 264KB of SRAM, 2MB of flash, and GPIO pins. The programming is CircuitPython, but the hardware tour applies directly to this chapter.
