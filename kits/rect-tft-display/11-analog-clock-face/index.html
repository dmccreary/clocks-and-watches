    Lab 11: An Analog Clock Face

    An analog clock shows the time with hands that sweep around a dial. This lab draws one: 60 tick marks, the numbers 1 to 12, and three moving hands. It also sets its own time from the internet.

    Welcome to Lab 11

    Now your kit looks like a clock you could hang on a wall. You will aim a hand at any spot on the dial and move it with no flicker. Let's make time tick!

    What You Will Learn

    • how to turn a time into an angle for each hand
    • how to move a hand without redrawing the whole screen
    • why the layout of a clock face matters to the program

    Run It

    1. Open 11-analog-clock-face.py in Thonny and click Run.
    2. The screen says Setting clock while it asks the internet for the time, as in lab 10.
    3. The clock face appears, and the red second hand starts to tick.

    No WiFi?

    Near the top of the program, change SYNC_WITH_WIFI = True to SYNC_WITH_WIFI = False. The clock then skips the internet and uses the time Thonny set.

    Every Hand Has an Angle

    A hand starts at the center and points out at an angle, which is how far it has turned. This clock measures angles clockwise from 12 o'clock. A full turn is 360 degrees.

    • The second hand takes 60 seconds for a full turn. So each second is 360 ÷ 60 = 6 degrees. The minute hand moves 6 degrees a minute.
    • The hour hand takes 12 hours, so each hour is 360 ÷ 12 = 30 degrees. It also creeps forward as the minutes pass.

    Check it on the picture at the top, which shows 10:10:

    Hand The math Angle On the picture
    Minute 10 minutes × 6 60 degrees right on the 2, which sits at 2 × 30 = 60
    Hour 10 hours × 30, plus one sixth of 30 for the 10 minutes 305 degrees just past the 10, which sits at 300

    def hour_angle(hour, minute):
    # The hour hand creeps forward as the minutes pass: at 3:30 it points
    # halfway between the 3 and the 4.
    return ((hour % 12) + minute / 60) * TWO_PI / 12


def minute_angle(minute):
    return minute * TWO_PI / 60
    • TWO_PI is a full turn. MicroPython's math measures angles in units called radians, and a full turn is about 6.28 of them. So TWO_PI / 60 is the same slice of the circle as 6 degrees.
    • minute / 60 is the creep. Thirty minutes adds half an hour.

    From an Angle to a Spot on the Screen

    To draw a hand, the program needs an x and a y for each end. A function named point() finds them. You give it a direction and a distance, and it answers with the x and y of that spot.

2

5
    def draw_second_hand(display, second, color):
    angle = second * TWO_PI / 60
    x0, y0 = point(angle, -SECOND_TAIL)
    x1, y1 = point(angle, SECOND_LENGTH)
    display.line(x0, y0, x1, y1, color)

    The hand is one line. It runs from a short tail, 9 pixels out the opposite way (-SECOND_TAIL), to the tip, 52 pixels out (SECOND_LENGTH).

    Inside, point() uses two math functions called sine and cosine. You will meet them in a later grade.

    Erase, Repair, Draw

    The Tricky Part

    This next idea is the hardest in the kit so far, and it is normal to read it twice. You did a small version in lab 07, when you repainted one box and left the rest alone. Take the three steps one at a time.

    Some screens have a frame buffer: memory where a program builds the whole picture before showing it all at once. This kit has none. Every shape goes straight to the glass, so drawing the whole face again each second would flicker.

    So the dial is drawn only once. After that, the program does three small jobs each second:

    1. Erase the old second hand by drawing it again in black.
    2. Repair the number that the black line cut through.
    3. Draw all three hands in their new places.

    It is like rubbing one chalk line off a chalkboard drawing. The eraser also wipes a bit of whatever the line crossed, so you draw that bit back.

    def update_hands(display, now, last):
    """Move the hands from `last` (hour, minute, second) to `now`.
    `last` is None the first time, when there is nothing to erase."""
    hour, minute, second = now
    if last is not None:
        last_hour, last_minute, last_second = last
        draw_second_hand(display, last_second, BLACK)
        if (hour, minute) != (last_hour, last_minute):
            draw_minute_hand(display, last_minute, BLACK)
            draw_hour_hand(display, last_hour, last_minute, BLACK)
        draw_numeral(display, numeral_under(last_second))

    draw_hour_hand(display, hour, minute, HAND_COLOR)
    draw_minute_hand(display, minute, HAND_COLOR)
    draw_second_hand(display, second, SECOND_COLOR)
    shapes.circle(display, CX, CY, CAP_RADIUS, SECOND_COLOR, config.FILL)
    • The first draw_second_hand(...) is the erase. It draws the old hand again in BLACK.
    • The minute and hour hands are erased only when the minute changes.
    • draw_numeral(...) is the repair. numeral_under() finds the number the old second hand lay across.
    • The last four lines draw the three hands and the red dot. The white hands are drawn every second, in case the black line cut through one.

    Only one number needs a repair because of where things sit on the dial. The tick marks are farther out than any hand reaches. The numbers are farther out than the hour and minute hands. So only the second hand ever crosses a number.

    Try This

    1. Change SECOND_COLOR = config.RED to config.CYAN. Why does the dot in the middle change too?
    2. In draw_numeral(), change WHITE, BLACK) to config.YELLOW, BLACK). The numbers stay yellow, even after a repair.
    3. Break it on purpose. Change SECOND_LENGTH = 52 to 60 and watch for a minute. The second hand now reaches the tick marks, and its black eraser line chews them up. Nothing repairs a tick. Change it back when you finish.

    If It Doesn't Work

    • The screen says Try 3 failed or No secrets.py found, and then the time is wrong. The clock could not reach the internet. Get lab 10 working, or use the tip above.
    • A number or a tick has a piece missing. A hand is too long. Set SECOND_LENGTH back to 52.

    Your Clock Has Hands!

    You turned hours, minutes, and seconds into angles. You moved three hands with no flicker: erase, repair, draw. Next, you will set the time by hand with the knob.
