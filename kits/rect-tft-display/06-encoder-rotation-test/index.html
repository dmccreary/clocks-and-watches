    Lab 06: Test the Knob's Rotation

    Turn the knob and you feel it click. How does the Pico know that it moved, and which way? This lab prints what the knob is doing, one tiny move at a time.

    A knob like this is a rotary encoder: it turns your twist into on-off signals that a computer can count. Here you check its two wires, called A and B.

    Welcome to Lab 06

    This knob can spin forever, with no start and no end, and it still knows which way you turned it. Today you learn to read its secret two-wire code yourself. Let's make time tick!

    What You Will Learn

    • how two switches inside the knob report a turn and its direction
    • what a step is (one small move of the switches), and why one click of this knob is two steps
    • why a fast spin can make a program miss a step

    Run It

    1. Open 06-encoder-rotation-test.py in Thonny.
    2. Click Run.
    3. Turn the knob clockwise, slowly, two clicks.
    4. Turn it back the other way, slowly, two clicks.
    5. Read the shell. Each click should print two lines.

    Two Switches, Four Positions

    Inside the knob are two switches, A and B. As the knob turns, they change one after the other, never both at once. It is like walking: left foot, right foot, never both together.

    So the pair (A, B) walks around a loop of four positions:

    Position A B The knob is...
    0 0 0 resting in a click
    1 1 0 halfway between clicks
    2 1 1 resting in a click
    3 0 1 halfway between clicks

    A clockwise turn goes down the table: 0, 1, 2, 3, then back to 0. A counter-clockwise turn goes up it. Each move to the next row is one step. The program sets this up first:

    1

    a = Pin(config.ENCODER_A_PIN, Pin.IN, Pin.PULL_UP)
b = Pin(config.ENCODER_B_PIN, Pin.IN, Pin.PULL_UP)

# The four positions of (A, B), in the order a clockwise turn visits them.
CLOCKWISE = ((0, 0), (1, 0), (1, 1), (0, 1))

pair = (a.value(), b.value())
last = CLOCKWISE.index(pair)
steps = 0
    • The first two lines set up A on GP8 and B on GP9. Each reads 1 or 0, like the button in lab 04.
    • CLOCKWISE holds the four rows of the table, in order.
    • pair is what both switches read right now.
    • CLOCKWISE.index(pair) finds that pair's position number, 0 to 3. last remembers it.

    Why Two Switches?

    One switch could only say "something moved." Two switches that take turns can also say which way. Notice that the order they change in is the message.

    Finding the Direction

    Look at your printed lines. Turning clockwise from rest, A changed first and B followed. Turning back, B changed first. The loop works that out in two parts. First, how far did the pair move?

    while True:
    pair = (a.value(), b.value())
    now = CLOCKWISE.index(pair)
    if now != last:
        # How far round the loop did the pair move? 1 is one step
        # clockwise, 3 is one step back, and 2 is a jump across the loop.
        move = (now - last) % 4
        last = now
    • now is the knob's position at this look.
    • if now != last: means "only go on if the knob moved."
    • move = (now - last) % 4 counts the positions moved, going clockwise. % 4 is the remainder after dividing by 4. It wraps the answer around the loop, the way a clock wraps from 12 to 1.

    Second, which way was that? These lines come next, inside the same if:

            if move == 1:

        else:

    A move of 1 is one step clockwise, so steps goes up. A move of 3 is one step back, so steps goes down. On a loop of four, three steps forward and one step back land in the same place.

    Try it on your first lines:

    Line printed Position move
    A=1 B=1 at rest 2
    A=0 B=1 clockwise 3 (3 - 2) % 4 = 1
    A=0 B=0 clockwise 0 (0 - 3) % 4 = 1

    Loop Math Takes Practice

    If (0 - 3) % 4 = 1 looks strange, that is normal. Put a finger on row 3 of the positions table and move one step clockwise. You wrap around to row 0, and that is all % 4 is doing.

    Two Steps for Every Click

    This knob clicks into place at position 0 and at position 2, where A and B match. One click moves it from one resting place to the next, which takes two steps. These lines, also inside the if, turn steps into clicks:

    1
2
3
            # Round to the nearest whole click.
        per_click = config.ENCODER_STEPS_PER_CLICK
        clicks = (steps + per_click // 2) // per_click
    • config.ENCODER_STEPS_PER_CLICK is 2 for this knob. Many knobs like it take 4, which is why it is a setting.
    • // divides and throws away the remainder. With 2 steps per click, the last line works out to (steps + 1) // 2.
    • The + 1 rounds a half-finished click up. That is why steps=1 and steps=2 both print clicks=1.

    Missed a Step

    The loop ends with time.sleep_ms(1), so it looks at the knob 1000 times a second. That is plenty for a slow turn. Spin the knob fast and you will see this:

    1
2
3
    A=0 B=0   clockwise           steps=2    clicks=1
A=1 B=1   missed a step       steps=2    clicks=1
A=0 B=1   clockwise           steps=3    clicks=2

    Between two looks, the pair jumped from position 0 to position 2. Did it go forward through 1, or backward through 3? There is no telling. It is like blinking during a magic trick. The program leaves steps alone, and the count is now wrong.

    Lab 07 fixes this with interrupts, which let the Pico notice A or B changing the moment it happens. The Rotary Encoder lesson in Learning MicroPython has more about this kind of knob.

    Try This

    1. Turn the knob exactly one click. How many lines print? Do A and B match on the last one?
    2. Spin the knob fast. How many missed a step lines can you get?
    3. Change time.sleep_ms(1) to time.sleep_ms(20). Turn the knob at normal speed. Does clicks still match the clicks you feel? Put the 1 back when you are done.
    4. Change per_click = config.ENCODER_STEPS_PER_CLICK to per_click = 4. How many clicks must you feel now before clicks goes up by 1?

    If It Doesn't Work

    • Nothing prints when you turn the knob. A and B are on the wrong pins. Check them against How It Is Wired.
    • Only A changes, or only B. The one that never changes has its wire on the wrong pin.
    • It says clockwise when you turn the other way. Swap the numbers of ENCODER_A_PIN and ENCODER_B_PIN in config.py.
    • clicks does not go up by one for each click you feel. Count the lines one click prints. Set ENCODER_STEPS_PER_CLICK in config.py to that number.

    You Cracked the Knob's Code!

    You can now explain how two switches report a turn and its direction. You also know why this knob takes two steps per click. Next, you will put K0, PUSH, and the knob on the screen together.
