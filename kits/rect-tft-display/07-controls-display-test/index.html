    Lab 07: Controls on the Screen

    Labs 04, 05, and 06 tested the controls one at a time, with words in the Thonny shell. This lab puts all three on the screen at once, so you can watch the kit react to your hands.

    What you do What the screen does
    Hold down K0 The K0 box fills with green
    Press the knob down The PUSH box fills with yellow
    Turn the knob The number counts the clicks, and the white marker slides

    Welcome to Lab 07

    This is the first time the screen answers your hands, and every later clock works the same way. You will also teach the Pico to keep count of the knob, even when you spin it fast. Let's make time tick!

    What You Will Learn

    • what an interrupt is, and why the knob needs one
    • why the two buttons get by without one
    • why an interrupt function should be short
    • how to redraw only what changed on the screen

    Run It

    1. Open 07-controls-display-test.py in Thonny and click Run.
    2. Hold down K0, the square button. Its box fills with green until you let go.
    3. Press the knob straight down. The PUSH box fills with yellow.
    4. Turn the knob one click clockwise. The number goes up by one and the marker moves one notch right. Turning back does the opposite.
    5. Spin the knob fast. In lab 06 that printed missed a step. This time the count keeps up.

    A Doorbell for the Knob

    In labs 04 to 06, a loop looked at the pins over and over. This loop has drawing to do, and it rests between trips. It comes around only about 50 times a second. A knob step that comes and goes between two looks would be lost.

    Think about waiting for a friend to visit. You could walk to the door every minute and look outside. A short visit between two looks would be missed. A doorbell is better. It tells you the moment someone arrives.

    An interrupt is a doorbell for a pin. You ask MicroPython to call one of your functions by itself, the moment the pin changes. It does not matter what the loop is doing right then.

    1
2
    a.irq(knob_moved, Pin.IRQ_RISING | Pin.IRQ_FALLING)
b.irq(knob_moved, Pin.IRQ_RISING | Pin.IRQ_FALLING)
    • a.irq(...) puts a doorbell on A, one of the knob's two switches. The second line does the same for B. irq is short for interrupt request.
    • knob_moved is the function to call when the bell rings.
    • The last part says when to ring: when the pin rises to 1, and when it falls to 0. That is every change.

    You may have met irq() in chapter 6 of Learning MicroPython, where it counted button presses.

    K0 and PUSH need no doorbell. A press lasts far longer than one trip around the loop, so the loop always catches it.

    Count Fast, Draw Later

    Here is the function the doorbell calls. It is the counting code from lab 06, moved into a function:

    1
2
3
4
5
6
7
8
9
    def knob_moved(pin):
    global steps, last
    now = CLOCKWISE.index((a.value(), b.value()))
    move = (now - last) % 4
    last = now
    if move == 1:
        steps += 1
    elif move == 3:
        steps -= 1
    • global steps, last lets the function change two variables that were made outside it.
    • now is which of the four positions A and B are in, from 0 to 3. last is where they were the time before.
    • move is how far around that loop of four the knob went. A 1 is one step clockwise. A 3 is one step back.

    The main loop turns steps into clicks, two steps to a click, as in lab 06.

    Notice What Is Missing

    Look at what knob_moved() does not do: it draws nothing and prints nothing. An interrupt cuts in on the rest of your program, so its function should write down a number and leave. The slow job of drawing waits for the main loop.

    Only Draw What Changed

    The main loop checks both buttons each time around. These lines, part of the program, run once for each button:

    1
2
3
4
    down = pin.value() == 0
if down != held[i]:
    held[i] = down
    draw_box(x, color, down)
    • down is True while the button is pressed. A pressed button reads 0, as lab 04 showed.
    • held[i] remembers what that button's box shows right now.
    • The if is true only at the moment those two differ. Only then is the box painted.

    Hold K0 for ten seconds and its box is painted once, not 500 times. Every lab after this one follows the same rule: redraw only what changed.

    Try This

    1. In BUTTONS, near the top of the program, change config.GREEN to config.RED. Which box changes color?
    2. Change NOTCHES = 20 to NOTCHES = 10. How many clicks does the marker need to cross the track now?
    3. Change the last line, time.sleep_ms(20), to time.sleep_ms(500). The loop now looks only twice a second, so it misses some quick taps of K0. Spin the knob. The number waits, then jumps to the right count, because the interrupt kept counting. Change the line back to 20.

    If It Doesn't Work

    • A box never fills. That control is not reaching its pin. Run lab 04 again for K0, or lab 05 for PUSH.
    • The number counts the wrong way, or not one per click. Lab 06 shows what to fix in config.py.
    • The screen stays blank. Go back to lab 03 and get the display working first.

    The Screen Answers Back!

    You gave the knob a doorbell with irq(), kept its interrupt function short, and redrew only what changed. Next comes a quick test of the light inside the screen.
