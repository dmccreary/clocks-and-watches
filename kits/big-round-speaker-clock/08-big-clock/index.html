    Lab 08: The Big Clock

    All the parts work. Now put them together: the biggest digits the round screen can hold, a ring of 60 ticks that counts the seconds, and a chime at the top of every hour. This is main-template.py, a clock you can leave running on your desk.

    Welcome to Lab 08

    You can read this clock from across the room, and it chimes to tell you the hour. By the end of this lab it will start by itself every time you plug it in. Let's make time tick!

    What You Will Learn

    • how to fit the biggest rectangle inside a circle
    • how digits are built from seven bars
    • how a program plays sound while it keeps the time

    Run It

    Open main-template.py and click Run. The clock joins WiFi, sets its time, plays a short start-up sound, and shows the time.

    Button What it does
    UP / DOWN Volume up or down, with a beep at the new volume. Hold to keep changing.
    tap MODE Turns the hourly chime on or off
    hold MODE Sets the clock from the internet right now

    Make It Start by Itself

    When a Pico powers up, it runs the file called main.py. Copy the clock there:

    1
    mpremote cp main-template.py :main.py

    Now unplug the Pico and plug it back in. It starts as a clock.

    Digits Made of Bars

    Each digit is a seven-segment digit, like the ones on a microwave: seven bars, each one either lit or not. The 8 lights all seven, and the 1 lights two. The unlit bars are drawn in a very dark gray, so you can see faint "ghost" segments behind every digit.

    How Big Can the Digits Be?

    A round screen is widest across the middle and narrower above and below. The row of digits is a rectangle, and its corners are what bump into the round edge first.

    A rectangle inside a circle

    A corner of the digit row is half its width across and half its height up from the center. By the Pythagorean theorem, that corner stays inside a circle of radius 150 when (width ÷ 2)² + (height ÷ 2)² is less than 150².

    Here the corners are 134 pixels across and 65 pixels up: 134² + 65² = 22,181, just under 150² = 22,500. It just fits.

    On a 12-hour clock the first digit is only ever 1 or blank, so the clock draws it as half a digit and gives the room it saves to the other three.

    Sound and Time Together

    Playing a sound means sending samples to the amplifier without a break. The kit's sound.py does that from the Pico's second core, the second processor inside the chip, so the first core can keep drawing the clock. The clock asks for a sound by name:

    1
2
    if chime_on and minute == 0 and not quiet:
    speaker.play("chime")

    quiet is True from 10 PM until 7 AM, so the chime never wakes anyone up.

    The Pico's own clock drifts a second or two a day, so every night at 3:00 AM the clock sets itself from the internet again.

    Try This

    1. Set TWELVE_HOUR = False for a 24-hour clock. Why do the digits get smaller?
    2. Change GHOST to config.BLACK to hide the unlit bars.
    3. Change "chime" to "doorbell" or "bird". sfx.py has more sounds to try.
    4. Change QUIET_START and QUIET_END to match when your family sleeps.

    If It Doesn't Work

    • MemoryError when the sound starts. Unplug the Pico and plug it back in, so it starts with fresh memory. If you changed the program, keep speaker.start() where it was: the sound needs one unbroken 4 KB block of memory, so it starts before the face is built.
    • No chime on the hour. Is the chime on? Tap MODE and read the note under the digits. Remember it stays quiet from 10 PM to 7 AM.
    • The time is wrong. Run Lab 07 to check the internet time.

    A Clock You Built!

    You fit the biggest digits a circle can hold, and your clock chimes while it keeps time. Put it on your desk: it sets itself every night from now on.
