    Lab 02: Twelve Dots in a Circle

    A clock face is a circle with things placed around it at equal steps. This lab places twelve black dots, one for each hour. It's a small program, but it hides the most useful trick in the whole kit: turning an angle into a spot on the screen.

    Welcome to Lab 02

    After this lab you can place anything at any angle around a circle. That's how every hand, every bar, and every dot on this clock gets where it's going. Let's make time tick!

    What You Will Learn

    • what a variable is
    • how a for loop repeats work
    • how sine and cosine turn an angle into x and y

    Run It

    Open 02-dial-dots.py in Thonny and click Run. Twelve dots appear around the screen, and Thonny's shell prints a line for each one:

    1
2
3
    hour 0 angle 0 dot at 180 30
hour 1 angle 30 dot at 255 50
hour 2 angle 60 dot at 310 105

    The first dot is at the top, at x = 180 and y = 30. Then the dots march clockwise.

    Variables: Names for Numbers

    The program gives a few numbers names, so the rest of the code can use the names:

    1
2
    DISTANCE = 150      # how far each dot sits from the center, in pixels
DOT_RADIUS = 7

    If you want the dots farther out, you change one number in one place. That is why programmers use variables.

    The For Loop

    1
2
3
    for hour in range(12):
    degrees = hour * 30
    ...

    range(12) counts 0, 1, 2, ... 11, twelve numbers. The loop runs the indented lines once for each number, and the variable hour holds the current one. A full circle is 360 degrees, and 360 ÷ 12 = 30, so each hour is 30 degrees farther around than the one before.

    From an Angle to a Spot

    A clock measures angles from 12 o'clock, going clockwise. Two math tools turn an angle into a position:

    1
2
    x = round(CENTER_X + DISTANCE * math.sin(angle))
y = round(CENTER_Y - DISTANCE * math.cos(angle))

    Why the minus sign on y? Remember from lab 01 that y grows downward. The minus flips it, so angle 0 points up at the 12.

    Sine and Cosine in One Sentence

    Think of standing at the center and walking DISTANCE steps toward a direction. Sine tells you how far you ended up across, and cosine tells you how far up, as fractions of your steps.

    One more detail: sin() and cos() want the angle in radians, not degrees, so math.radians() converts it first. round() turns the result into a whole number of pixels, since there's no such thing as half a pixel.

    Strange-Looking Math?

    Most people don't meet sine and cosine until high school, so if they feel strange, that's normal. You already placed things by x and y in lab 01, and these two tools just turn an angle into an x and a y.

    Try This

    1. Change range(12) to range(60) and hour * 30 to hour * 6. Now there's a dot for every minute.
    2. Change DISTANCE to 100. Where do the dots go?
    3. Make every third dot red. (Hint: if hour % 3 == 0:. You'll meet % in lab 04.)

    A Circle of Dots!

    You used a for loop, sine, and cosine to place twelve dots at exact angles. Next, you'll turn those dots into the bold bars of a Swiss railway clock.
